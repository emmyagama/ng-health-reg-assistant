## Reproducibility statement

A stranger reproduces the corpus with one command:
`python download_corpus.py`. It fetches each of the 8 documents from the URL recorded in `data/sources.json`, refuses anything that is not a real PDF (HTTP status, Content-Type, magic bytes and a 10,000-byte floor), then confirms the file really is the document it claims to be by looking for at least two document-specific phrases in its text. Documents whose text layer is too thin to check are OCR'd automatically into `data/ocr/` before the check runs, so no manual step is required. `python download_corpus.py --verify` re-checks every local file against the committed sha256 in `data/corpus.lock.json` and exits non-zero on any mismatch. If a URL ever stops working, its entry is marked with `"acquisition": "manual"` plus the exact expected sha256, and the document must be placed in `data/raw/` by hand — that is the only step that cannot be automated, and this table records whether it applies today (currently: 8 of 8 automated by URL, 8 of 8 content-verified).

The fetcher sends a browser User-Agent. On the observation date, 1 of 8 sources refused the default python-requests User-Agent: `who_lab_quality_guidance` (HTTP 403). Do not remove `USER_AGENT`.
